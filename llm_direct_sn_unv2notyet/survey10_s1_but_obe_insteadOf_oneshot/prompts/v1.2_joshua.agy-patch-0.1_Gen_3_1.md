## Root Cause Analysis

The oscillation across multiple outputs was not caused by a failure to comprehend the Hebrew text, but by the lack of hard constraints when mapping Hebrew tags onto Chinese syntactic structures. The process was treating Strong's Number (SN) placement as an under-constrained semantic guessing game on every pass, rather than a strict mapping exercise anchored to a definitive source inventory. 

Because the process lacked explicit boundary rules for target-language function words and failed to enforce a strict tag count, it constantly second-guessed itself in three highly specific, structurally ambiguous zones:

1. **Genitive Linkers (`的`):** There was confusion over whether a noun's tag should attach strictly to the lexical head (`樹`) or to the entire possessive phrase (`樹的`). Without a definitive policy, the placement flipped symmetrically.
2. **Verb-Aspect Compounds (`說過`):** The process failed to consistently recognize when a Chinese aspect particle (`過`) was an integral part of the translated verbal unit carrying the Hebrew morphology (such as the perfective `<8804>`). This led to unnatural splitting of Chinese compounds.
3. **Repeated Surface Words (`蛇`):** A breakdown in count discipline. The source text contained the tag for "serpent" (`<05175>`) exactly once, but the target Chinese translation restated the noun for clarity. Reasoning from the Chinese surface rather than the source inventory, the process sometimes erroneously duplicated the tag onto the second occurrence.

Ultimately, the regressions occurred because there was no "halt condition." Without strict policies to lock in particle boundaries and verify the exact tag inventory, the process attempted to "improve" an already correct output, destabilizing it instead.

## Instructions for Strong's Number Placement

To guarantee accurate, stable Strong's Number placement and prevent oscillation, you must treat the source text as an authoritative mathematical constraint. Apply the following rules sequentially and strictly.

### 1. The Multiset Invariant (Strict Inventory Control)
Before placing any tags, extract the exact inventory of Strong's Numbers (including morphology tags) and their exact frequencies from the UNV source.
*   **1-to-1 Conservation:** Your final output MUST contain exactly the same tags, in the exact same quantities, as the source text. 
*   **Repeated Words:** If a Chinese word appears multiple times in a sentence (e.g., a restated subject) but the corresponding Hebrew word appears only once in the source, **tag exactly one occurrence**. Map the tag to the primary aligned occurrence and leave all subsequent target-language re-mentions completely untagged. Do not duplicate tags just because the Chinese word is duplicated.

### 2. The Semantic Unit Policy
Strong's Numbers must attach to the right side of the smallest complete Chinese semantic unit that translates the Hebrew concept. You must distinguish between lexical heads and verbal compounds.

*   **Rule 2A: Connective/Structural Particles (`的`, `之`, `所...的`)**
    These particles are structural linkers, not parts of the word. Attach the tag strictly to the **lexical head** (the content word) *before* the structural particle.
    *   *Correct:* `樹<06086>的` (Tag the noun, leave the linker outside).
    *   *Incorrect:* `樹的<06086>`

*   **Rule 2B: Verb-Aspect Compounds (`了`, `過`, `著`)**
    When an aspect or modal particle completes the meaning of a verb and reflects the Hebrew morphology (e.g., a perfect/qatal `<8804>` tag), treat the verb and particle as a single, indivisible semantic unit. **Do not split the compound.** Place the tag group *after* the entire verbal unit.
    *   *Correct:* `說過<0559><8804>` (Tag encompasses the verb + aspect).
    *   *Incorrect:* `說<0559><8804>過`

### 3. Execution and Convergence
*   **Align First:** Align the text by semantic meaning first to establish the mapping between source units and target units.
*   **Halt Condition:** Once a generated output exactly matches the source tag inventory (satisfying the Multiset Invariant) and adheres flawlessly to the Semantic Unit Policy (Rules 2A and 2B), **ACCEPT IT AND STOP**. Do not re-evaluate, re-roll, or attempt to semantically "improve" an output that has satisfied these hard constraints.
